252,279 fire hotspots detected across Kalimantan so far in 2026.

That is 8.9x the same period last year. And 93% of them appeared between August and 12 October.

Behind each dot on the map is forest, peat, wildlife, and the air millions of people breathe. Central Kalimantan alone accounts for about half. Reported burned area already exceeds 89,000 hectares (BNPB, four provinces, to 14 September), larger than the land area of Jakarta.

I built Visualize Karhutla to make this visible and easy to share: an interactive hotspot map, a yearly "Wrapped" story for every regency in Kalimantan, and Instagram Story templates for awareness.

A note on honesty: hotspots are satellite heat detections, not fires and not hectares. The deck explains what the data can and cannot say, and every figure is sourced (NASA FIRMS, BNPB via CNBC Indonesia and Katadata).

It is open source. Journalists, data people, and illustrators are welcome to contribute articles, data, corrections, or artwork.

The fires are not over. If this moved you, send it to one person who should see it.
